# Review

A reviewer who did not make the visual checks it against the source. A figure is a set
of claims: every element either matches the paper or is labelled schematic or
illustrative.

## Checklist

1. **Value.** Does the visual express the one idea its notes state? Would a reader
   understand that idea faster or more correctly with it? If not, recommend removal.
2. **Notation.** Every symbol is the paper's, defined in the caption or on screen.
3. **Signs, orientation, faces.** Incoming versus outgoing boundary, the direction of
   time, which faces belong to a domain, what a reflection preserves.
4. **Numbers and factors.** Exponents, constants, factor-two slips, the stated range
   of each parameter.
5. **Exactness.** What is claimed exact is computed; displayed constants satisfy the
   inequality being illustrated (check the inequality itself, not the look of ordered
   curves).
6. **Consistency.** A curve drawn as a derivative is the derivative of the curve drawn
   beside it; compared profiles are compared at the same time and on a common scale;
   dimensions agree with the source.
7. **Bounds versus data.** Plotted estimates are the paper's uniform upper bounds, not
   fits; illustrations are labelled; projections are identified.
8. **Readability.** Legible at text width (and, on a website, in both themes and at
   phone width); curves distinguishable; formulas not crammed into the drawing.
9. **For animations:** every symbol defined on screen; banners say what and why;
   essential steps get time; chapter titles match the argument; claimed agreements are
   measured.

Record each finding and correction in the figure notes ("corrected after review: …"),
so a later revision does not reintroduce it.

## Author feedback patterns

Authors reviewing finished figures repeatedly asked for:

- the reading order of the argument (reorder panels or number the steps);
- dense formulas moved out of the picture into the caption or an expandable detail;
- higher contrast and an individual label for each curve;
- the decisive sentence made prominent next to the picture;
- more time on the essential moments of an animation, and larger on-screen text;
- illustrative choices kept visibly labelled.

Anticipate these before review.
